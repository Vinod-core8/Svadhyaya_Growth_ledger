Reach · 17–23 Aug

Pure value, zero pitch — six Reels, six different hook techniques.

Mon 17 Aug
HookReelThought-Provoking Question
Why Accountancy feels like a different language
HookWhy does Accountancy suddenly feel like a different language?
Build-Up"Every other Commerce subject reads like English with new vocabulary. This one reads like code — debit this, credit that — and nobody tells you why."
Value"Here's the fix: stop treating Debit and Credit as 'good' or 'bad.' They're just directions — left side, right side. Nothing moral about them."
Payoff"Once you stop hunting for meaning in the words themselves, the rules stop feeling arbitrary — and a lot of your fear of this subject disappears with it."
CTAFollow for more shifts like this one.
Tue 18 Aug
ConceptReelMyth-Busting
What is Capital, actually?
HookCapital isn't "the owner's money." Not really.
Build-Up"Every student assumes Capital just means whatever the owner put in. That's only half true — and the other half is what trips people up in exams."
Value"In accounting, the business and the owner are treated as two separate entities — even in a one-person shop. So Capital is what the business owes back to the owner."
Payoff"That's why Capital sits on the liabilities side — not because it's a debt in the everyday sense, but because the business is accountable to whoever funded it."
CTAThis one idea unlocks half the confusing entries in this chapter — save it.

Thu 20 Aug
ConceptReelControversial Mixup Callout
Solvent vs Insolvent vs Bad Debt
HookThese 3 words get mixed up more than almost anything else in this subject.
Build-Up"And it's not because they're hard — it's because they sound like they should mean similar things. They don't."
Value"Solvent — can pay debts, assets exceed liabilities. Insolvent — can't, liabilities have overtaken assets. Bad debt — different altogether, it's one customer not paying, not the business's overall health."
Payoff"Mixing up 'insolvent' and 'bad debt' specifically is the #1 slip-up here."
CTAComment which one you always confuse.
Fri 21 Aug
ConceptReelSurprising Rule
Current Assets — the 12-month rule
HookThere's a 12-month rule hiding in this definition that most students miss.
Build-Up"You'd think 'Current Asset' is about what the asset IS. It's actually about something else completely."
Value"It's about how soon it turns into cash. Cash, stock, money owed to you — if it converts within 12 months, it's Current."
Payoff"Same 12-month test we'll use for liabilities in a few weeks — just flipped to the assets side."
CTASave this — it comes back in the next chapter too.

Receivables vs Payables
HookWhich one are you confusing right now — Receivable or Payable?
Build-Up"Everyone learns the definitions. Almost nobody can identify them fast in an actual transaction — and that's what the exam actually tests."

Payoff"Customer hasn't paid you for last month's sale — Receivable. Got that in 2 seconds? You've got it."

Fictitious Assets, actually explained
Hook"Fictitious Assets" — scariest name, simplest concept in this book.
