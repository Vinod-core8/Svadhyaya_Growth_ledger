Mon 17 Aug
HookReel
Why Accountancy feels like a different language
On-Screen Text (0–2s)
Why does Accountancy suddenly feel like a different language?
Script

"Every other subject in Commerce — Economics, Business Studies — reads like English with new vocabulary. Accountancy reads like code. Debit this, credit that, and none of it explains why."

"Here's the one shift that fixes it: stop thinking of Debit and Credit as 'good' or 'bad.' They're just directions — left side, right side. Once you stop hunting for meaning in the words themselves, the rules stop feeling arbitrary."

Caption
This one shift in how you think about Debit and Credit changes everything else in this subject. Follow for more.
Tue 18 Aug
ConceptReel
What is Capital, actually?
On-Screen Text
Capital isn't "the owner's money" — it's a little stranger than that
Script

"Capital isn't just 'the owner's money.' It's what the business owes back to the owner — because in accounting, the business and the owner are treated as two separate entities, even in a small shop."

"That's why Capital sits on the liabilities side of the equation. Not because it's a debt in the everyday sense, but because the business is accountable to the person who funded it."

Caption
This single idea — business and owner are separate — explains half the confusing entries in this chapter.

Thu 20 Aug
ConceptReel
Solvent vs Insolvent vs Bad Debt
On-Screen Text
3 terms, one exam trap
Script

"Solvent means a business can pay its debts — assets are more than liabilities."

"Insolvent means it can't — liabilities have overtaken assets."

"Bad debt is different altogether — it's not about the business, it's a specific customer who's not going to pay what they owe."

"Mixing up 'insolvent' and 'bad debt' is one of the most common slip-ups in this chapter."

Caption
Three words, three very different meanings. Comment which one you always mix up.

HookReel
The re-reading trap
On-Screen Text (0–2s)
Read it 3 times. Still doesn't make sense?
Script

(Shweta, sitting with a textbook, half-smiling like she's heard this before)
"If you've read the same line in your Accountancy book five times and it still doesn't click — it's not you. It's how it's been explained."

(Cut to a notebook, quick handwritten example)
"Take Discount and Rebate. Everyone mixes these up. Discount is what you reduce before the bill is even made — like a 10% off tag. Rebate is what you give back after — like a refund for buying in bulk. That's it. That's the entire confusion, solved in ten seconds."

(Back to camera)
"Most 'hard' topics in this subject are actually one-line ideas dressed up in scary language."

Caption
Real talk — most of you don't have an "Accountancy problem." You have a "nobody explained it simply" problem. Save this before your next test.

Tue 25 Aug
ConceptReel
Receivables vs Payables
On-Screen Text (0–2s)
Receivable or Payable? Most get this wrong in 2 seconds.
Script

"A customer buys from you on credit — they owe you money. That's a Receivable."

"You buy from a supplier on credit — you owe them money. That's a Payable."

"Quick trick: Re-ceivable, Re-ceive. If 'receive' is hiding in the word, money's coming toward you."

(On-screen quick test) "Customer hasn't paid you yet for last month's sale — which one?" (1-second pause) "Receivable."

Caption
The exam doesn't test the definition, it tests whether you can spot it in a transaction. Comment your answer before I said it out loud — I'll test you first next time.

Thu 27 Aug
ConceptReel
Fictitious Assets, actually explained
On-Screen Text (0–2s)
"Fictitious Assets" — scariest name, simplest concept
Script

"Preliminary expenses. Say that five times fast — and you'll understand why students panic the moment they see 'Fictitious Assets.'"

"Here's all it means: money a business spends before it even starts earning — registration, legal fees, setup costs. Since it's unfair to dump all of that cost into Year 1's profit, we spread it across a few years. That spreading is why it sits under 'Assets' — even though it isn't really something the business owns."

"Not fictional. Not confusing. Just… spread out."

Caption
The name is doing all the intimidating. The concept takes 30 seconds. Which term scares you the most? Tell me and I'll break it down next.
